How the studio is built

Doctrine, then practice, then failure

Every module runs the same three beats. First the provision — its exact ingredients, punishment, and cognizability. Then a video lesson slot where your faculty recording sits. Then a drill where you apply it to facts that are deliberately ambiguous, because the offence usually turns on the ambiguous element: dishonest intention, actual knowledge, or whether the person had authority.

The forensics lab is not decoration

Lawyers lose electronic evidence cases on procedure, not on doctrine. The lab teaches the six procedural competencies a cyber law practitioner is expected to cross-examine on: hashing and integrity, order of volatility, file signature verification, chain of custody, the section 63 certificate, and the capability envelope of the standard tools. You will not be running the tools — you will be learning what a report from each one can and cannot prove.

Jurisdiction. This studio is built on Indian law. Where a provision has been renumbered by the 2023 criminal codes — Evidence Act section 65B becoming Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam section 63, IPC 420 becoming BNS 318 — both numbers are shown, because your examiners and the reported judgments still use the old ones.
Verify the bare act. Statutory text is amended and rules are notified constantly. Treat this studio as a teaching aid, never as a citable source. Check India Code and the judgment itself before you put anything in a memo.

Forensics lab

Six workbenches covering the procedural ground a cyber law practitioner is expected to know. Each one ends with the cross-examination question it prepares you to ask.

1 · Integrity workbench

Hashing, SHA-256, tamper detection

A hash function turns any quantity of data into a fixed-length value. Change one bit of the input and roughly half the output bits flip. That property is why an investigator computes a hash at the moment of seizure and again before production: if the two agree, the record before the court is bit-for-bit the record that was taken.

Type anything below and watch the digest change completely. Then set it as the seizure hash and make a small edit.

SHA-256—
Referencenot set
MD5 and SHA-1 are still in the case files. Older Indian forensic reports use MD5. Both algorithms have demonstrated collisions, so a defence can argue that a matching MD5 does not conclusively establish identity of content. It is rarely fatal, because a collision must be engineered in advance, but it is a legitimate line of attack. SHA-256 is the current standard.

What a hash does not prove

  • It does not prove who created the file or who wrote its contents.
  • It does not prove the date. Timestamps live in the filesystem metadata, not in the hash.
  • It does not prove the seizure itself was lawful.
  • A hash computed after the device left the investigator's control proves only that nothing changed since that later moment.
Cross-examination. "At what precise time was the first hash generated, and who else had physical access to the device between seizure and that computation?"

2 · Order of volatility

RFC 3227 · what to collect first

Evidence decays at very different speeds. Collect in the wrong order and the most fragile material is destroyed by the act of collecting the durable material. Pulling the plug on a running machine, for example, is a defensible choice for disk preservation and a catastrophic one if the encryption keys were only in memory.

Click the sources in the order you would collect them, most volatile first.

    3 · File signature inspector

    Magic numbers vs. extensions

    An extension is a label typed by a user. The real type of a file is written in its first few bytes. Renaming a video to .docx hides it from a casual look at the folder but not from a forensic tool, which reads the header. This is how concealed material is routinely found, and how a "corrupt file" defence is defeated.

    4 · Chain of custody builder

    Seizure to production

    The chain is the documented history of every person who held the exhibit and every act done to it. A gap in the chain does not automatically make evidence inadmissible in India, but it goes directly to weight and invites the inference that the record could have been altered. Assemble the sequence for a seized laptop.

      Statutory hook. Section 176(3) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 requires a forensic expert to visit the scene and collect evidence in offences punishable with seven years or more. Read it alongside the search and seizure powers in section 185 BNSS and the power to summon production of electronic communication devices in section 94 BNSS.

      5 · Section 63 certificate clinic

      Formerly section 65B, Evidence Act

      A computer output is admissible as a document without producing the original device, provided the conditions in section 63(2) are satisfied and a certificate under section 63(4) is filed. The certificate is not a formality. Its absence has sunk entire prosecutions.

      Read each proposed certificate and decide whether the exhibit gets in.

      The four conditions in outline

      • The computer producing the output was in regular use for activities carried on by the person having lawful control over it.
      • Information of that kind was regularly fed into it in the ordinary course of those activities.
      • The computer was operating properly throughout, or any malfunction did not affect the accuracy of the record.
      • The information in the output reproduces or derives from information fed in during the ordinary course.
      The certificate must be signed by a person occupying a responsible official position in relation to the operation of the relevant device or the management of the relevant activities. It must identify the electronic record, describe the manner of production, and give particulars of the device.
      Line of authority. Anvar P.V. (2014) held the certificate mandatory. Shafhi Mohammad (2018) relaxed it where the party did not control the device. Arjun Panditrao Khotkar (2020) overruled Shafhi, restored Anvar, and clarified that a party unable to obtain the certificate may apply to the court under the production powers of the procedural code.
